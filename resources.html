Tool 02Araç 02

Exam study plannerSınav çalışma planlayıcısı

Twenty-eight examinations, from GRE and LSAT to YDS and a school term grade. Tell it where you are, where you need to be, and how many hours a week you can genuinely give. It returns a realistic number of weeks and where those hours should go — the same arithmetic I use in a first meeting.

GRE ve LSAT'ten YDS'ye ve okul dönem notuna kadar yirmi sekiz sınav. Nerede olduğunuzu, nerede olmanız gerektiğini ve haftada gerçekten kaç saat ayırabileceğinizi söyleyin. Gerçekçi bir hafta sayısı ve bu saatlerin nereye gitmesi gerektiğini verir — ilk görüşmede kullandığım hesabın aynısı.

  13. Describe a habit you would like to break.
  14. Describe a photograph you remember clearly.

Structure that fills two minutes: what it was → when and where → why it happened → what you thought at the time → what you think now. The last two are where band 7 candidates separate from band 6.

WritingYazma Essay and statement structuresDeneme ve niyet metni yapıları IELTS Task 2 · statement of purposeIELTS Task 2 · amaç beyanı

IELTS Writing Task 2 — four paragraphs, 40 minutes

  1. Introduction (3 sentences, 5 minutes). Rewrite the question in your own words; state your position in one clear sentence; name the two things the essay will argue. Do not announce “In this essay I will…”.
  2. Body 1 (6–7 sentences, 12 minutes). One idea. Topic sentence → explain the mechanism, not just the claim → one concrete example → one sentence linking back to your position.
  3. Body 2 (6–7 sentences, 12 minutes). The second idea, or the counter-argument answered. If you concede something, concede it properly and then say why it does not change the conclusion.
  4. Conclusion (2 sentences, 5 minutes). Restate the position in different words and give the implication. No new argument, no “To sum up, in conclusion, finally”.
  5. Check (6 minutes). Articles, subject–verb agreement, and whether every paragraph actually answers the question asked rather than the topic mentioned.

Statement of purpose — five paragraphs

  1. The moment. One specific thing that happened, told in three or four sentences. Not “I have always been passionate about…”.
  2. What you did about it. The study, the project, the work. Evidence, not adjectives.
  3. What you could not do yet. The gap — this is what makes the application necessary rather than optional.
  4. Why this programme specifically. Two named people, courses or facilities. A paragraph that would work for any university is a paragraph that works for none.
  5. What you will do with it. Concrete, five years out, and consistent with paragraph one.

For teachersÖğretmenler için Erasmus+ and eTwinning starter checklistErasmus+ ve eTwinning başlangıç listesi first project, step by stepilk proje, adım adım

Written from running eTwinning projects with schools in Spain, Poland, Georgia, Romania, Germany, Italy and Portugal, and from coordinating Erasmus+ across 179 schools in one district.

İspanya, Polonya, Gürcistan, Romanya, Almanya, İtalya ve Portekiz'deki okullarla yürütülen eTwinning projelerinden ve bir ilçedeki 179 okulda Erasmus+ koordinasyonundan yazıldı.

  1. Register on the platform first, project second. Get the school and yourself verified before you have an idea — verification is the step that delays everyone.
  2. Write the outcome before the activities. What will exist at the end that does not exist now: a book, an exhibition, a dataset, a recording. Activities are chosen to produce it.
  3. Two partners, not eight. A first project with two committed schools finishes. One with eight loses half of them by month three.
  4. Agree the calendar in the first month, in writing, including national holidays in each country. This is the single most common cause of a stalled project.
  5. Assign a student role in every activity. Projects that are teacher-run do not earn quality labels.
  6. Document as you go. Photographs with consent, minutes of every online meeting, and one paragraph of reflection each month. Assembling this at the end never works.
  7. Read the quality-label criteria at the start, not when applying. They tell you exactly what evidence to collect.
  8. Plan the dissemination. School website, local directorate, parents' meeting, a short article. Dissemination is assessed and is the part most projects skip.
